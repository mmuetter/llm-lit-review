## Abstract

Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) and nanoplastics (NPs) are emerging aquatic contaminants. This study examined the individual and combined effects of HFPO-DA ammonium salt (GenX, 10 μg/L), virgin polystyrene nanoplastics (PS-NPs, 1 mg/L), and aged PS-NPs (1 mg/L) on the submerged macrophyte Vallisneria natans over 30 days. GenX significantly inhibited growth, reducing leaf and root biomass by 22.1% and 42.7%, and lowering chlorophyll a, b, and carotenoids by 20.7%, 35.1%, and 23.2%, respectively. PS-NPs alone slightly enhanced plant growth and pigment levels. Notably, co-exposure with PS-NPs attenuated the observed toxic effects of GenX, as reflected by improved growth performance and reduced GenX accumulation in plant tissues. This reduction is likely due to enhanced adsorption by aged PS-NPs with larger surface areas and lower zeta potentials. GenX and PS-NPs triggered a physiological stress response in V. natans, significantly reducing MDA concentrations while elevating antioxidant enzyme activities (SOD, CAT, and POD), but PS-NPs attenuated these effects. Ultrastructural damage and PS-NPs accumulation were observed on plant surfaces, more so with aged PS-NPs. Microbial analyses revealed GenX and PS-NPs decreased Proteobacteria and increased Bacteroidota and Firmicutes. These findings suggest an antagonistic effect at the organismal level, and provide process-level explanations rather than direct mechanistic evidence for the interactions between PFAS and nanoplastics.
